AI for linguistics
← Back to the tools

Recent advances in AI have generated a great deal of discussion in linguistics, much of it about what the success of large language models can (or cannot) tell us about the human capacity for language. Beyond its interest as an object of study, however, AI is changing how research itself is done. In mathematics, for example, an existing culture of computational formalization, with proof assistants and large libraries of formalized results, gives AI systems a way of having their output checked automatically, and researchers have used this to make progress on long-standing problems.

Many linguists are already using AI tools, mostly on an ad hoc basis and without shared norms. We propose an open discussion of how these tools can and should fit into linguistic research. Which areas of linguistics, if any, have the kind of formal substrate that makes AI output checkable, and what would it take to build more of it? (We start with semantics which plausibly has the right structure, as well as because of our own backgrounds.) Do the culture and established norms of our field pose particular challenges or offer particular opportunities compared to other sciences? What are the ethical implications?

We are committed to explore the situation of AI for linguistics. This includes sharing useful resources and may evolve into other structures for discussing use, challenges and opportunities of AI for linguistics. We hope to offer a forum for open discussion of how to move forward and build knowledge, methods, best practices and goals we can reach as a community.

Who are we: We explore ways to facilitate, accelerate, automate, transform and scale different parts of research in semantics. Contributors include Emmanuel Chemla, Robert Hawkins, Patrick Elliott, and Shane Steinert-Threlkeld. We do not take responsibility for the use of these tools, some are experimental and require great caution. We nonetheless hope that they may prove useful in the right circumstances and, above all, inspire new ideas and ways of working.
